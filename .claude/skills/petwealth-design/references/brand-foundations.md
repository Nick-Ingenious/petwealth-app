# Brand foundations

Source of truth for anything not covered here: `Marketing/Brand/Petwealth Brand Guidelines (3 20 26).pdf` (83 pages) and the read-only Brand Guidelines Figma file (`CRxbpBGRXWKNcZBXbXJbU2`). Never edit the guidelines file.

## Positioning and voice

- **What we are:** a pet health-intelligence platform. Premium black magnetic box, three gradient sample-collection kits (Fecal, Oral, Respiratory), swab tubes, and an app that returns a health score and findings. Moving pet care from reactive to proactive.
- **Tone:** "emotional intelligence" and "silent luxury". Petwealth creates harmony between heart and science, a brand that feels as human as it is intelligent. Quiet, confident, warm. Never loud, cutesy, fear-mongering or salesy. Full voice guide: `voice-and-social.md`.
- **Approved lines:** "Love, backed by science." / "For those who trust you most." / "Health is wealth. Love is the return." / "Prevention is priceless." / "Pet longevity is the new luxury." / "Gold-standard diagnostics, made simple."
- **Boilerplate (approved):** "Petwealth makes premium at-home health testing for dogs and cats. Our vet-grade kits screen for fecal, oral, and respiratory pathogens. Fast results in 24-48 hours and clear reports, so pet parents understand what's going on and bring answers to their vet."
- We help people "bring answers to their vet". The vet is a partner after results. Never "diagnose", never position against vets.

## Color

**Core palette**

| Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Slate | `#19242B` | Anchor: dark backgrounds, primary type |
| Teal | `#0E4655` | Support, depth, header bands |
| Stone | `#F8F2EA` | Warm neutral background |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Backgrounds, type on dark |
| Lilac | `#CAB3FF` | Accent, used sparingly (pills, highlights, editable cells) |

How to use them (from the guidelines): **Slate** is the anchor for type, key backgrounds and graphics. **Teal** brings depth, large background areas and key brand moments. **Stone** softens layouts and creates space. **White** is used generously so other colors breathe. **Lilac** is used sparingly to inject energy or highlight a detail, complementing rather than competing.

- Stone appears as `#F8F2EA` in the main guidelines and `#FAF6F1` in the social playbook; either reads correctly, but match the file you're working in.
- **Standard dark surface:** a 180° gradient `#19242B → #143E4A` (slate to deep teal). The brand mesh gradient is for highlights only; don't decorate every asset.
- **Highlight words** draw subtle attention without overpowering. The guidelines show gradient highlights; current work mostly uses a solid color instead: lilac on dark, teal on light, purple `#7D61C7` in emails, or the kit color for a test name. Gradients also bring warmth to pet imagery. Never on the logo.
- **Traffic-light palette** (red, amber, yellow, light green, green, each with 75 to 900 tints) is strictly for interface states: diagnostics, risk levels, metrics, success, warning, error. Never decorative.

## Typography

- **Creolia**: display serif for headlines and big numbers. File: `Marketing/Brand/Fonts/creolia-25504707/Creolia.ttf`.
- **Plus Jakarta Sans**: body, UI, labels. Files in `Marketing/Brand/Fonts/.../Plus_Jakarta_Sans/static/`. Web: Google Fonts (one variable file covers weights 200 to 800).
- **Gelica** (Medium / Medium Italic / Black) + **Google Sans Flex**: the type pairing in many proven paid static ads. Match whatever an existing ad file uses. Use Google Sans Flex, not the local-only "Google Sans".
- In Figma, fonts only reach automation if uploaded to the account (Settings > Account > Your uploaded fonts); desktop installs don't count.
- Creolia is expressive and used sparingly for impact: headlines, hero statements, big numbers. Plus Jakarta Sans does everything else (body, captions, UI, chips, CTAs), Regular / Medium / Bold.
- **Type scale (at 1080 to 1920px canvases):** Display 150px · H1 100px · H2 75px · H3 60px · H4 36px, all tracked -3%. Body 24px at -2%. Captions 20px at 0%. Sans UI text tracks about -1%.
- **Track headlines tight, never loose. One display moment per asset; never two competing headlines.**

## Logo

- Files: `Marketing/Brand/Logos/` (e.g. `LOGO-WHITE@2x.png`), nose/heart icon `NOSE-ICON-*`.
- The full lockup (icon + wordmark, fixed relationship) is the primary logo. The wordmark alone is for limited space. The icon alone is used sparingly for small-scale or digital uses, never in place of the full logo. Approved color combinations only, with the clear space shown in the guidelines.
- Minimum widths: full logo 50px, icon 12px (some pages show 24px; go bigger when in doubt).
- Don't: recolor outside the brand palette, rotate, add gradients or shadows, use low-res files, or retype the wordmark in a font.

## Photography direction

**Overall register:** editorial stillness, soft light, real connection. Think A24, Apple, Oura, Aesop, Loewe, never pet retail. "Every image should feel considered, not created."

**Do**
- Pets are the focal point; humans are context (partially out of frame is fine).
- Candid, unposed moments with genuine connection (touch, eye contact between pet and person).
- Natural or soft directional light, soft contrast, neutral stone and slate palettes, clean compositions with breathing room, shallow depth of field.
- Comfortable, lived-in settings (homes, parks) or controlled studio for the "editorial + clinical luxury" look: focused, observant moments, slightly serious, side profiles.
- Even, accurate tones across a set. Show vitality and personality.
- "Clinical overlays in real life": a pet with subtle data UI, informational, not playful.

**Don't**
- Make the human the hero, crop tightly on human faces, or use direct-to-camera staged poses.
- Over-dramatize emotion, use sad dogs, comical or animated poses, forced smiles.
- Harsh lighting, high saturation, low exposure, heavy edits, stretching, low quality.
- Busy, cluttered, chaotic or high-energy compositions; bright colors and props; cartoon styling; trend-driven or gimmicky content; overly artificial AI renders.

**Photography by channel:**
- **Paid ads:** candid, UGC-style photography (phone-shot homes, natural light, imperfect framing) performs best, so lean into it. Still no chaos, clutter, sad pets or staged smiles, and the pet stays the hero. See `ads-static.md`.
- **Email and website (including PDPs and landing pages):** aim a little more editorial. Polished but natural, soft light, clean compositions, studio or considered home settings, June 2026 shoot imagery first. Real customer or UGC photos still fit inside testimonial and review modules.
- **Organic social, decks and brand moments:** the full editorial register described above.

**Product and packaging photography:** restraint and intention. Clean, uncluttered compositions, natural light, soft shadows, neutral environments. In lifestyle scenes the kit integrates into the moment rather than dominating it. Avoid anything that feels like a traditional ad: harsh light, heavy graphics, excessive props, floating product shots, overly polished "ecommerce-style" images, packaging forced into frame. "Quiet. Refined. Confident. Nothing loud, nothing gimmicky, nothing that tries too hard." (PDP and catalog imagery are the functional exception, see `web-landing-pdp.md`.)

**AI imagery** is acceptable when it looks realistic and natural. Prompt formula: *cinematic editorial photograph of [subject] + [composition] + [environment] + [lighting] + [lens] + [mood] + [brand modifiers]*. Example: "cinematic editorial photograph of a golden retriever resting its head gently on its owner's leg, minimal modern interior, soft directional natural light, neutral stone and slate tones, shallow depth of field, shot on 85mm lens, quiet luxury, calm and observant mood, pet is the clear focal point, human partially out of frame." When translating a loud reference image, keep its idea and convert it to quiet luxury + clinical.

**Where assets live:** product and lifestyle finals in `Marketing/Photography/June 2026 Photoshoot/` (transparent PDP renders in `2ND ROUND TRANSPARENT PNGS`); packshots in `Marketing/Photography/PetWealth Primary Product Photos/`.

## UI and app visuals

- Clarity and coherence: rounded corners, balanced spacing, subtle transparencies.
- **Glass textures** (frosted translucent cards) add depth while keeping focus on the pet. Layer UI cards so they interact with the pet photo without cluttering it or covering the face.
- **Icons:** Streamline "sharp solid" style, used consistently.
- **Buttons:** consistent shapes, weights and color states; primary buttons stand out with confidence, secondary and tertiary support. Light and dark mode variants exist.
- **Line illustrations:** a single continuous line, consistent stroke weight, no shading or extra detail, on core brand color backgrounds. Use sparingly to support content.
- Health categories in UI: Fecal/Digestive, Oral/Dental, Respiratory (plus future Systemic/Immune, Urinary, Environmental/Allergy). In customer-facing copy still say fecal, oral, respiratory.

## Shared components

**Dark card (decks, one-pagers, dark web sections)**
- Card fill `#222F37` on Slate `#19242B`, corner radius 24 (at 1920px slide scale), no stroke.
- Titles white; body `#B8C2C9` at 150% line height; per-test accents use the lightened kit colors above.

**Price / value pill**
- Horizontal auto-layout, fully rounded (radius 999), padding 8 / 15, Lilac fill, Plus Jakarta Sans Bold 13px, 1px letter spacing, Slate text.
- Copy pattern `$AMOUNT LABEL`: "$249 VALUE", "$139 MSRP".
- Outlined variant (no fill, 1.5px lilac stroke, lilac text) for non-price labels.
- Scale 1.328x when moving from a 1920 slide to a 2550px portrait one-pager.

**Savings pill on a card** (PDP quantity tiles, cart, email product cards): small pill reading e.g. "SAVE $56 WHEN YOU BUY 2". Check the channel file for exact placement, which varies by surface.

**Promo seal** (sale periods): lilac `#CAB3FF` circle with highlight, Creolia offer ("20% / off"), curved Google Sans Flex text around the edge, rotated about 10 degrees, placed in a photo's empty space. Mirrors the live PDP `.pw-promo-seal`.

**Checkmark lists**: one benefit per line, sized generously. On light ad layouts, green filled circles with white ticks; on PDP slides and dark or kit-color backgrounds, lilac circles with dark ticks.
